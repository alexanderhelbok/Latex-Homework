\section*{Reading summary}

As the title suggests, \blockcquote{devoret_superconducting_2013}{Superconducting Circuits for Quantum Information: An Outlook} gives a superficial perspective on quantum computation using the superconducting circuits platform. The authors explain past efforts leading up to today and then go on to describe their vision for how future research in this area will progress. \blockcquote{gao_coherent_2015}{Coherent manipulation, measurement and entanglement of individual solid-state spins using optical fields}

Superconducting circuits as a potential platform for quantum computation emerged in the late 1990s and have since been at the forefront of modern research. They leverage \textbf{superconductivity} and the \textbf{Josephson effect} to create \textbf{anharmonic LC oscillators} with quality factors in the range of \( \mathbf{10^6} \) resulting in lifetimes of \( \sim \mathbf{100 \unit{\micro\s}} \). State of the art lithography pioneered by the semiconductor industry is used to \textbf{pattern the electrical circuit} onto a wafer forming capacitive and inductive elements (which make up the qubit) that are connected via transmission lines. \textbf{Microwave signals} can then be used for qubit manipulation and readout. 

The \textbf{outstanding feature} of superconducting circuits (for me) lies in their flexibility. Whilst most other platforms rely on \blockquote{naturally occurring} two-level systems, the \textbf{two-level structure} of superconducting qubits is an \textbf{emergent property} of the electrical circuit design (which is why they are often referred to as \textit{artificial atoms}). This has both \textbf{advantages} and drawbacks. On the one hand, we can \textbf{tune parameters} such as qubit frequency or coupling strength to suit ones need. On the other hand, being to manipulate such fundamental properties implies that they are susceptible to drift caused by thermal fluctuations, silicon degradation, etc. As a result, they need to be frequently characterized to account for the changed properties and thus maintain performance.

Quantum dots and nitrogen-vacancy centers appear promising as \textbf{interface} layer between \textbf{stationary and flying qubits} due to the strong \textbf{spin-photon interaction}. In this way quantum information can be \textbf{interchanged} between \textbf{photonic polarization} and \textbf{spin states} enabling long-range entanglement as well as the connection between spatially separated qubit complexes. The underlying mechanism is \textbf{spin-orbit coupling} between a trapped spin and an incident light field. Trapping spins is frequently realized by confining electrons/holes in self-assembled quantum dots or in nitrogen-vacancy centers, which are atomic-scale defects in diamond that can host isolated electron spins.
